<objective>
Build and seal the held-out fixture split, and the mechanical injectors that produce
its defects, BEFORE the remaining two repair rules are written.

Purpose: D-13-25 is the sharpest finding in the discussion and this plan is built
around it — the overfittable artifact in Phase 13 is not the driver (`repair.go` has
no kind-to-edit table to overfit), it is `check`'s choice of WHICH FUNCTION'S SPAN
the repair points at. So the split must vary call-graph topology and
detection-to-fix hop distance. A held-out corpus that varies identifiers is
ceremonial; one that varies topology is not. M001's own corpus is the cautionary
case: its held-out and derivation members are near alpha-renamings and the shipped
control asserts only byte-inequality, which any rename passes.

Output: the phase-13 injectors with their guards and not-inert twins, the full
held-out/derivation corpus including D-13-28's twin pair in BOTH halves, the
topology-disjointness control with its own mutation kill, and the sealed
`HELDOUT.sha256` manifest.

**Ordering is the point.** This plan runs in wave 2, before plan 13-05 writes the
`wrap_call_in_try` and `use_matching_argument` rules. APR's overfitting literature
(Smith et al., FSE 2015) is why the split must be drawn before the repairs are
written, not after.

**Discretion settled by the planner, recorded here:** new corpus directory
`testdata/phase13/` (not an extension of `testdata/phase6/`); NO `isPhase13Corpus`
sibling is added to `cmd/lang/main.go`, because this corpus is consumed by Go tests
and by per-file `lang --json check`, never by `lang verify <corpus>` — instead a
guard test asserts the directory carries no filename that `cmd/lang/main.go`'s
existing marker dispatch would swallow. The topology triple is re-derived
independently over `ast.Program` in the test rather than read from the callgraph
package, per the peer-re-derivation discipline.
</objective>
