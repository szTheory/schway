<objective>
Close CR-01's source-layer half: make a `data` declaration whose alternatives
collide on payload type a named, fail-closed refusal instead of a silently
mis-resolved program.

Purpose: `12-VERIFICATION.md` scores truth 2 as FAILED (partial) for exactly one
reason. Every other payload hazard this phase introduced — arity mismatch,
missing binder, binder on a nullary alternative, resource-carrying payload — gets
an explicit refusal. The colliding-payload-type shape gets silence, and then
`cgen.alternativeNameForPayloadType` (cgen.go:1939) and
`interp.alternativeNameForPayloadType` (interp.go:839) each independently resolve
the operation's declaring alternative by first-match linear scan. Because both
engines reimplemented the SAME flawed derivation, the phase's own three-engine
convergence tests are structurally incapable of catching it — they would report
cheerful agreement on a wrong answer. D-12-12 makes payload-carrying alternatives
a GENERAL `data` feature, so the hazard is reachable, not hypothetical, and
`session.PayloadProbeDataType` (session.go:612-620) already constructs exactly
the colliding shape as a legal `core.DataType`.

Output: `check.duplicate_payload_type`, a committed refusal fixture, a control
that fails if the refusal is reverted, a measured corpus-neutrality proof, and
WR-01's diagnostic-message precision fix.
</objective>

<cr01_disposition>
**Recorded decision — CR-01 disposition is FIX, not ratified debt.** This is stated
here explicitly rather than left implicit in the existence of this plan, because
`12-VERIFICATION.md`'s human-verification item 1 routed the *disposition itself*
to a human and the two branches were "committed fix + re-verification" versus
"explicit debt entry with a named landing condition". The FIX branch is taken.
Recorded as decision **D-12-44** (entry authored in plan 12-08, Task 2).

**Which of the verifier's two candidate closures, and why.** The verifier named
(a) a fail-closed `check.*` diagnostic, and (b) carrying an `AlternativeName`
fact directly on the operation so resolution never round-trips through
`PayloadType`. The choice is **(a) here, plus a narrowed form of (b)'s intent in
plan 12-07** — collapsing the two duplicated derivations into ONE shared,
ambiguity-detecting resolver — rather than (b) as literally described.

Reasoning, in order of weight:

1. **(b) as literally described is an IR schema change.** Adding a field to
   `core.LinearOperation` touches the JSON wire shape, `corevalidate`'s replay
   laws, all six dispatch sites, and the explicitly-enumerated field set that
   `core_convention_absence_test.go`'s `linearOperationExpectedFields` pins as a
   tripwire. `<must_not_regress>` requires `TestPayloadCorpusCharacterizationReplay`
   to stay byte-identical across the whole pre-Phase-12 corpus; a new IR field is
   the single change in this space most likely to move those bytes. A check-time
   refusal cannot move them, and Task 2 measures that rather than assuming it.

2. **(a) is provably fail-closed and matches the phase's own documented
   pattern.** Four sibling hazards, four named refusals. This is the fifth. The
   verifier's own gap text cites that pattern as the argument against silently
   accepting CR-01.

3. **(a) alone is NOT sufficient, and this plan does not claim it is.** Leaving
   two copies of an ambiguous first-match derivation in two engines is the exact
   defect class D-12-25 ("layout is ONE shared derived fact, not three
   independent derivations") exists to prevent, and `check`'s refusal only
   governs programs that crossed the parser — `session.PayloadProbeDataType`
   proves a `core.DataType` can be hand-built in Go and never sees `check` at
   all. Plan 12-07 therefore collapses both copies into one shared resolver in
   `core` that reports ambiguity loudly instead of returning first-match, and
   also absorbs IN-01's third local reimplementation. That is (b)'s *intent* —
   resolution stops being an ambiguous re-derivation — without (b)'s IR-schema
   blast radius.

4. **Ordering is deliberate.** The refusal lands first (this plan, wave 1) and is
   independently verifiable on its own — Task 2's control asserts the diagnostic
   code directly and does not depend on anything plan 12-07 does. Plan 12-07's
   engine-layer work then becomes an unreachable-by-construction backstop rather
   than the primary closure, which is the correct risk ordering.

<assumption_delta_decision>
The detector fired (`detected: true`, two `pluralization` signals, terms
`alternative` and `second`). Answering the identity-model question it poses:

- **Noun now primary:** `PayloadType` remains the primary identity for "which
  alternative is this operation about". It is NOT promoted away in favour of the
  alternative name or the operation's own `TargetID`.
- **Decision:** `no-change`.
- **Rationale:** the pluralization signal is real — "a second alternative with
  the same payload type" is exactly the shape CR-01 is about — but the response
  chosen is to make that shape *inexpressible* (refused at declaration time)
  rather than to promote a new identity fact onto the operation. Promoting
  `AlternativeName` to a `core.LinearOperation` field is the IR-schema change
  rejected in `<cr01_disposition>` point 1, on corpus-byte-stability grounds.
- **What would force a later promote:** a future requirement that legitimately
  needs two alternatives to share one payload type (GEN-01's real generic
  `Result<T, E>` in M003 is the obvious candidate — `Ok(T)`/`Err(T)` at
  `T == E` collides by construction). At that point the refusal added here is the
  thing that must be lifted, and lifting it REQUIRES promoting the alternative
  name to a first-class operation fact. That relationship is recorded so the
  refusal reads as a scoped restriction with a named exit, not a permanent law.

Advisory and non-blocking; recorded, not gated.
</assumption_delta_decision>
