<objective>
Close verification gap 3 (04-REVIEW.md CR-01): give `checkReleaseOrder`'s
`rederive` backward walk the same visited-set guard its two sibling walks in the
same file already carry, refuse a re-visited block with a new
`core.release_order_cyclic` code rather than truncating or hanging, and add the
falsifier that proves both — the walk RETURNS, and it REFUSES.

Purpose: `corevalidate` exists to rederive facts independently of `check.go`, on
a `core.Program` it does not trust. Two of its three graph walks say so in their
own doc comments: `loanChainIndex.carriedLoans` ("precisely the kind of
adversarial input a source-blind validator must stay defined against") and
`blockReach` ("terminates in bounded time even over an (illegitimately) cyclic
declared graph"). `rederive` is the one walk without the equivalent guard. Its
loop's only exit is the `okEdgeInto[currentBlockID]` lookup missing; because
`okEdgeInto` is built directly from `linear.Edges` and no acyclicity check runs
anywhere earlier (`blocksAndEdges`, corevalidate.go:376-476, checks ID uniqueness
and referential closure only), a cycle of `ok`-pattern edges makes that lookup
succeed forever. The validator hangs — an unbounded denial of service on exactly
the adversarial input class independent validation exists to defend against, and
untested (zero `cyclic`/`Cyclic` matches in `corevalidate_test.go`).

What this plan does NOT do: it does not add an acyclicity precheck to
`blocksAndEdges`, does not touch the `core.terminal_block_unreachable` check
04-08 added, and does not change `okEdgeInto`'s construction. The gap is a
missing guard on one walk; the fix is that guard and its falsifier.

Output: the guard plus refusal in `corevalidate.go`, two tests in
`corevalidate_test.go`, one Mutation-Kill Register row.
</objective>
